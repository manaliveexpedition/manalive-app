Week 7 overview

What this week is for

Week 7 walks a man back through Saturday's last session before lunch, An Adventure to Live. Not a bucket list and not a career change: the start of finding his place in the Larger Story. One clue at a time, with the Father. By Sunday he should be able to say:

I have a place in the Larger Story. My life matters, and God is willing to guide me. My story, my desires and my journey are clues. Following Him will mean some risk. Adventure is walking with God into the life He's giving me. And I'm starting to ask: Father, what are You inviting me into?

What Did You Want?

Reading

Remember the kids in that commercial? Saturday, right before lunch. Little kids looking straight into the camera. When I grow up, I want to file all day. I want to claw my way up to middle management.

The room laughed. Then it got quiet.

It's funny because kids said it. It's not funny because so many men live there.

Nobody was knocking your job. Work pays the bills. Responsibility is a good thing. But somewhere along the way a lot of us learned to survive, to meet expectations, to take care of what had to be done. And life got smaller. Not all at once. A little at a time.

The battle sessions that morning ended on this: the battle isn't the whole story. There's still an adventure to live.

So this week starts with one question from that room. What did you want before you learned to settle?

If you can't remember, that's okay. Your story isn't finished.

Bigger Than a Weekend

Reading

Say the word adventure in a room full of men and half of them see a deer stand. A river. A motorcycle.

Those are good. Hunting, fishing, hiking, a road trip with your buddies. At camp they got a name: casual adventures. They wake something up in you. Nobody was knocking them.

But adventure is bigger than a weekend.

There are crucial adventures, where your heart is on the line with another person. Going after your kid's heart. Having the conversation you've been avoiding. Going after a friend who's slipping.

And there are critical ones. The kind where you can't control how it ends, and God has to come through. Those look different for every man.

Here's the point under all three. In Week 2 you found the red dot. You are here. This week goes one step further. You're not just in the story. You have a part in it.

Your life matters. And the One telling the story is willing to show you.

Other Forces at Work

Reading

Remember Frodo in the dark of the mines? Lost. Worn out. Done.

He says what every man says at some point: I wish none of this had happened.

Gandalf doesn't argue with him. He reminds him of the story. There are other forces at work in this world, Frodo. Bilbo was meant to find the Ring. Which means Frodo was meant to have it.

Watch Frodo's face. He doesn't get answers. He gets his bearings back. And that's when hope comes back.

That's the first clue to your place: story. What story are you actually living in?

A lot of men live in a story they made up. This is how my life is supposed to go. Some live in a story somebody handed them. Be what your dad was. Don't rock the boat.

And then there's the Larger Story God is actually writing. It has more going on in it than you can see from where you're standing.

The One Thing

Reading

Remember Curly? The old cowboy in City Slickers holds up one finger. The secret of life. One thing. Just one thing.

And Mitch asks what every man in the room wanted to ask. What's the one thing?

That's what you have to find out.

Nobody at camp said there's one secret job out there and you'd better find it. Your calling goes deeper than your job, your skills or your personality test. The one thing is a question, not a formula.

That's the second clue: desire. What makes you come alive? What do you keep wanting to bring into the world?

Here's the catch. Not every desire comes from the same place. Some come from the flesh. Some from old wounds. Some from the enemy. And some from the deep, good heart God gave you.

So don't bury desire. Don't worship it either. Bring it to Dad.

On the Way

Reading

The third clue is journey.

You've probably met a man whose life you'd trade for. The way he talks. The way he leads. Something in you says, I want that. Why don't I have it yet?

Here's what you don't see. The road he took to get there. The failures. The training. The years nobody clapped.

At camp, the question was simple: What is God doing in me right now?

Maybe He's building character. Maybe He's bringing clarity. Maybe He's teaching you to wait. Maybe He's healing something. Maybe He's giving you rest.

A man can know something about his desire and still not be ready for the whole assignment. That's not a problem. That's the journey. Nobody skips it.

So if you're in a season that feels slow, or stuck, or nothing like what you wanted, hear this. Training isn't failure.

You're not late. You're on the way.

Walk With Him Into It

Reading

Near the end of the session, one more man stood up and told his story. Probably nothing like the speaker's. That was on purpose.

Some adventures look like a moving truck. Some look like staying when you want to run. Caring for a parent. Being the dad your dad wasn't. Taking one hard next step.

Every real adventure has risk in it. Something you can't control. That's what makes it an adventure.

But risk isn't recklessness. It's not quitting your job to feel alive. It's not walking out on the people counting on you. Dramatic doesn't mean holy.

Here's the center of the whole session: adventure is walking with God into the life He's actually giving you. Not God hands you a mission and you go run it alone. You walk with Him. You listen. You take the next step together.

So the adventure might be going. It might be staying. It might be waiting.

If you're walking with Him, it's already started.

Decisions made in this draft

  • One piece of the session per day. Day 43 wakes the heart (Monster commercial), Day 44 is Casual, Crucial, Critical kept short and landing on "you have a part," Days 45 to 47 are the three clues (Story, Desire, Journey), Day 48 is risk and walking with God (the responder moment), Day 49 lands on "Father, what are You inviting me into?"
  • Discovery opened, not completed. No day tells a man what his calling is, equates calling with career, or suggests quitting a job. Desire is never "if you want it, God put it there": Day 46 names flesh, wounds, the enemy and the deep good heart, then "bring it to Dad."
  • Current media only: Monster commercial, City Slickers, Fellowship of the Ring. Dead Poets, Bagger Vance and Zorro left out per the spec. The broadsword picture on Day 47 comes from Gary's Boot Camp talk, used as an image, not as a camp moment. Cut it if it doesn't fit how you teach.
  • "A ship in harbor is safe" (John A. Shedd) opens Day 48's audio. It's in the 2024 and 2025 talks, not the spec, so it's in your voice rather than named as a camp moment.
  • No speakers named. Speakers change camp to camp, so the readings point to the clips and the responder moment, never to Reed's, Steve's or Joshua's stories.
  • No Covenant of Silence. Current practice sends men to lunch carrying the questions, and Day 49 says so. The 2019, 2024 and 2025 talks did send men into silence after this session. Confirm the lunch line works for alumni of those camps.
  • Staying out of Beauty's lane. Crucial adventure examples are a kid's heart, a hard conversation, a friend slipping; pursuing a wife is left for next week. Day 49's bridge question is "Why does beauty get to me the way it does?" Confirm it.
  • Five story slots (Days 43, 44, 46, 47, 48). Days 45 and 49 use non-story devices: the "what do you complain about" test and an ordinary-Monday walk-through. No held stories were assigned to this week. The jail story isn't invited; it doesn't fit Adventure's lane.
  • Day 48, please confirm: the 2019 Texas transcript has a responder introduced as John Pulley telling about leaving roofing sales and driving a U-Haul north to Eau Claire, Wisconsin, without knowing if he'd be paid, and his biggest fear being losing face. If that's yours, it's a strong fit for the slot. It isn't written in, because I couldn't confirm it.
  • Trumpet callback used once, on Day 46 ("Back in Week 1, I asked you, what's your trumpet?"). The trumpet story itself isn't retold.
  • With Dad is 5 minutes on weekdays, 10 on Saturday, 3 on Sunday.
  • Audio times assume each story runs about 30 seconds. A longer answer pushes that day past 2:00; the script around it is already trimmed.
