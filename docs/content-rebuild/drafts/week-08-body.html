Week 8 overview

What this week is for

Week 8 walks a man back through A Passion for Beauty, so he stops asking a woman to answer what only Dad can answer, and starts bringing strength instead. Single, married, divorced, or widowed, it stays with his heart, not his relationship status. By Sunday he should be able to say:

My desire for beauty is not the enemy. I can see where I've asked women to answer questions that belong between me and Dad, and where that has tangled my heart. There may be soul ties I need to break. My failures don't have to be the end of my story. Dad is where I get my deepest validation and strength. The woman in my life has a story that matters, and I don't have to fix her. Jesus is her rescuer, and I can partner with Him. I'm learning to bring strength instead of taking it. Fierce for her. Tender with her.

Day 50 · MondayAudio about 2:05

Not the Problem

Reading

Remember how the Beauty session started? Somebody stood up and read Song of Songs out loud. The whole passage.

And the room got loud. Laughing. A couple of catcalls. Some guys studying the floor.

That's in the Bible. On purpose.

Here's why it was read: the pull you feel toward a woman's beauty was God's idea. So is the pull toward a mountain, or a song that stops you cold. It isn't the broken part of you.

That room had single guys, married guys, divorced guys, widowers. And the session wasn't about any of those situations. It wasn't a marriage talk or a dating talk. It was about your heart.

Because the pull isn't the problem. The problem starts with what you ask her to give you.

This week we walk back through that session. Not to shame you. To get you free.

Wherever you are with women right now, this week is for you.

Carry this
The pull isn't the problem.
Audio script John

I'll take a risk on almost anything.

A lot of days a week, I go get beat up on a jiu-jitsu mat. It scares me some. I go anyway, because I want to get better.

You heard me tell about packing everything I owned into a U-Haul and driving to Wisconsin with no idea where I'd live or how I'd get paid.

Risk? I'm in.

But there's one area where I've had a real hard time risking. Women.

The pull toward them? Easy. But how to move toward a woman without needing something from her? For a long time I had no clue. I used to say women were crazy. They're not. They're made in the image of God. I was the one who didn't know what to do with my own heart.

Here's what took me years to see. The desire was never the problem. God put it there. He made you to be moved by beauty. A sunrise. A song. A woman.

The problem is what we ask it to do. Make me feel like a man. Tell me I'm okay. Make the ache go away. That's a weight no woman was built to carry.

So this week, we're not killing the desire. We're untangling it.

And if you're single, divorced, or you've lost your wife, stay with me. This is about your heart, not your relationship status.

So here's what I want you to carry today. The pull isn't the problem.

When you feel it today, don't panic and don't feed it. Thank God for how He made you. Then ask Him the honest question: what have I been asking it to give me?

Go talk to Dad about it.

With Dad 5 minutes

Thank Him for the way He made you, pull and all. Then ask: Father, what have I been asking beauty to give me? Listen. If nothing comes, that's okay. You're just starting to look.

Day 51 · TuesdayAudio about 1:55

Sweet Vermouth

Reading

Remember Groundhog Day? Phil is stuck living the same day over and over. So he works it.

Every day he learns one more thing about Rita. Her drink: sweet vermouth, rocks, with a twist. What she toasts to. What she studied in college. The next day he plays it all back to her like it's fate.

The room laughed. Then it got a little quiet.

Because Phil knows everything about her and doesn't know her at all. He's not asking, who is she? He's asking, how do I get her to tell me something about me?

Every man carries two questions. Do I have what it takes? Am I the apple of my Father's eye? Those were made to be answered by Dad.

When we take them to a woman, even a good one, she becomes the answer key. And she can't be.

Today, just notice. When you move toward her, what are you hoping she'll tell you?

Carry this
What am I asking her for?
Audio script John

In high school I wasn't the athlete. I was the band geek. That's where I got my sense that I was okay: performing, looking good in front of people.

With girls, I knew my looks weren't going to carry me. So I figured some things out. Girls like to laugh. So I got funny.

Then I figured out something better. Most guys don't listen. So I listened. I'd let her talk, and I could feel myself go up a notch in her book.

Now, listening is good. Making somebody laugh is good. She felt seen. She had fun.

But years later I had to face it. That's where my manipulation started. I couldn't have said it then. But I wasn't listening to know her. I was listening to get something. I wanted her to tell me I was okay. I wanted my two questions answered.

That's Phil in Groundhog Day. All the right moves, for the wrong reason.

And even when it works, it doesn't work. A woman can't hand you your masculinity. She can love you. She can encourage you. But she can't answer the question at the bottom of your heart. That one belongs to your Father.

So here's what I want you to carry today. What am I asking her for?

Not to beat yourself up. Just to notice. When you crack the joke. When you check whether she's looking. When you need her to be happy with you. Ask it.

And then take the real question to the One who can actually answer it. Dad, do I have what it takes?

Ask Him. Then listen.

With Dad 5 minutes

Ask Him: Father, where have I asked a woman to tell me who I am? Then: What do You say about me? Listen. No beating yourself up. Just honest.

Day 52 · WednesdayAudio about 2:05

The Report Card

Reading

After Groundhog Day, the session turned the camera around. On us.

Because most of the ways a man looks to a woman to make him okay aren't sexual at all.

Needing her mood to be good before your day can be good. Dreading that she's disappointed in you. Dodging the hard conversation because her reaction feels like a verdict. Needing to be wanted. Getting flattened when she says no. Single guys know this one too: needing her to pick you.

There was a question at camp worth hanging on to: Is she someone I'm free to love, or has she become the report card on whether I'm okay?

That's a lot to hand somebody. And it isn't fair to her.

Here's the turn. What you've been demanding from her belongs between you and Dad. Get it from Him. Then you're free to walk toward her with something to give, instead of something to get.

From needing to bringing.

Carry this
She's not my report card.
Audio script John

About six months into dating my wife, we were sitting outside Walmart waiting on our groceries. And I said, honey, I need to tell you something. It's not going to sound very romantic at first. Stick with me.

I said, I don't need you.

You don't complete me. You're not my better half. I've got my work, my friends, my ministry. I'm a whole person.

But every day of my life, I choose you. Because I don't know anybody like you. And I think you'd rather be chosen than needed.

She didn't say much the rest of the day. Her first thought, she told me later, was not a nice one.

The next morning she brought it back up. She said she'd been with a man who needed her to make him feel good about himself, all the time, and she was worn out from carrying it. And she said, I love that you don't have to have me to be okay.

I could only say what I said because I'd started getting my answers somewhere else. From Dad. From good men.

That's the freedom. When she's not your report card, you can actually love her. You can stay in the hard conversation. You can let her have a bad day without it meaning you failed. You can bring something instead of needing something.

So here's what I want you to carry today. She's not my report card.

No woman in your life right now? It still counts. Maybe it's an ex who still grades you in your head. Maybe it's every woman who didn't pick you.

Take the report card back to Dad. Ask Him what He thinks of you. His answer is the one that sticks.

With Dad 5 minutes

Ask Him: Dad, what would it look like for me to get my validation from You? Then listen. Write down whatever He says about you, even if it sounds too good to be true.

Day 53 · ThursdayAudio about 2:10

Tangled

Reading

Some of us are still carrying somebody.

An old girlfriend. An ex-wife. An affair. A hookup. A woman whose approval, or rejection, or memory still has a grip on you years later. You moved on. Your heart didn't quite.

At camp we called that a soul tie. It's ManAlive's word for a relationship, past or present, that still holds power over your heart it shouldn't have.

Not every past relationship leaves one. And loving or missing someone isn't one. A widower's grief is not a tangle. This is about the holds that keep pulling you where you don't want to go.

Remember the Covenant of Silence? An hour alone with the handout. There was a prayer on it: Jesus, I break the soul tie between myself and ___.

Maybe you prayed it. Maybe you didn't get to it. Maybe no one came to mind. All okay.

That prayer is still yours. It's between you and Jesus. Nobody else needs a name.

Carry this
Give it back. Take it back.
Audio script John

I'll be honest with you. Soul ties still sound a little weird to me.

The first time I heard about them was at my first boot camp. They sent us out into the woods to break soul ties with every woman we'd been involved with. I didn't know any better. So I wandered through the woods saying it out loud. I break the soul tie with Susie. I break the soul tie with Marla. I break the soul tie with that girl at the party.

It wasn't earth-shattering. But as I said it, something lifted. Just a little.

Then toward the end, a voice in my head said, you know this is bullshit. You're going to go right back home, and everything's going to be the same.

And right there in the woods I cried out to God. No. I can't do this on my own. I've got to have You.

Over the years, it's become one of the most valuable things I do. Women I've dated. Friendships. Family. Releasing that tie breaks off something that had been feeding off my soul. And the lightness that comes after? I know it now. I've felt it too many times to doubt it.

I still tell guys, this is going to sound weird and feel weird. But it works.

Here's what I know now. When you hand your heart to someone, again and again, looking for something only Dad can give, it doesn't just leave when the relationship does. It hangs on. A memory you keep going back to. A comparison you can't shake. A voice that still tells you whether you're enough.

You don't want to carry that into the rest of your life. Into your marriage, your next relationship, or your quiet nights alone.

That's what the prayer is for. Listen to the end of it again. Anything I have taken from her, I return now. Anything she has taken from me, I take it back.

Those aren't magic words. It's you and Jesus putting things back where they belong.

So here's what I want you to carry today. Give it back. Take it back.

And hear me, this is not a beatdown. You don't need to make a list of everyone you've ever dated. Just ask Dad if there's anyone. If a name comes, take it to Jesus, alone, and pray the prayer. If no name comes, that's an answer too.

Give it back. Take it back. Then let Him have your whole heart.

With Dad 5 minutes

Ask Him: Father, is there anyone my heart is still tangled with? If a name comes, pray the prayer from camp, just you and Jesus: Jesus, I break the soul tie between myself and ___. I bring the cross of Christ between us. Anything I have taken from her, I return now. Anything she has taken from me, I take it back. If no name comes, that's okay. Thank Him and stay a minute.

Day 54 · FridayAudio about 2:15

Not the End of the Story

Reading

Remember Kingdom of Heaven? Balian has already blown it. He slept with Sibylla, the king's sister, a married woman.

Then the offer comes again. Bigger this time. Marry her, command the army, have the kingdom. All he has to do is let a man be killed to get it.

And he says no.

That's why the scene is there. Not because Balian was a perfect knight. Because he wasn't, and he still turned.

Some men in that room had blown it badly. Porn. An affair. Using women. Disappearing when she needed him. Maybe that's you.

Hear this: shame has never set a man free. Repentance isn't beating yourself up or white-knuckling it. It's turning around and walking back toward Dad. Then small choices, one at a time, that give your strength back.

Your worst failure doesn't get to write the rest of your story.

Carry this
Not the end of my story.
Audio script John

I want to talk to you about failure, because I know it well.

I had affairs when I was married to my first wife.

The first time, I was wrecked. Guilt everywhere. I swore I'd never do it again.

The second time was easier. Less guilt.

By the third time, I didn't care.

That's how it goes when you keep giving your strength away. A little more goes each time, and the next time costs less. I wasn't bringing strength to anybody. I was trying to take it. Because underneath it all, I didn't believe I was worth anything. I felt like a failure.

I'm not telling you that so you'll feel worse about whatever you've done. I'm telling you because that was not the end of my story.

What came next was my world falling apart. When I got caught, the marriage ended. The job ended. Everything changed.

The turn started at that first boot camp Darrell dragged me to. I had zero expectations that God was going to say or do anything.

And then He spoke to me. You've heard me tell about it. He told me I was His son. Not because of anything I'd done or hadn't done. I'm loved whether I do the right thing or the wrong thing. Loved. Period.

That freed me in a way I still don't have words for. And it started me down the road of learning what it means to be a beloved son. That road has brought so much healing into my life, and it still does.

Look at Balian. He didn't stay where he fell. When the next offer came, he said no. And every no makes the next one easier. Strength comes back the way it left. One choice at a time.

Repentance isn't proving to God you'll be good from now on. It's turning around, and letting Him give back what you gave away.

So here's what I want you to carry today. Not the end of my story.

If you've failed here, and most of us have in some way, don't hide from Dad today. Ask Him where you gave your heart away, and what turning looks like now. He's not keeping score. He wants you back.

With Dad 5 minutes

Ask Him: Father, where have I given my heart away, and what would repentance look like now? Then listen. No beatdown. If He puts His finger on one thing, that's enough for today.

Day 55 · SaturdayAudio about 2:25

Her Story

Reading

After Kingdom of Heaven, the session turned again. From what you want from her, to what's going on in her.

She has a story too. Things that happened to her. Messages she picked up along the way. Questions she carries: Am I lovely? Do you delight in me?

You can't answer those all the way down. Only God can. But how you look at her, talk to her, show up or disappear, matters more than you know.

Knowing her story isn't so you can figure her out or tell her what's wrong with her. It's so you know the terrain. Sometimes the moment isn't about you. It's about something much older.

And she's not your project. Jesus is her rescuer, not you. You get to partner with Him as He comes for her heart.

That goes for a wife, a daughter, your mom, a sister, a friend. Every woman in your life is somebody's daughter. His.

Carry this
Know her. Don't fix her.
Audio script John

Most of us have a move when a woman we love is hurting. We fix it.

She starts telling you what's wrong, and before she's done you've got three solutions. And if she doesn't take them, you get frustrated. Because somewhere in there, her being upset feels like you failing. We're back to the report card.

Your story

Tell about a time you learned something from a woman's past that explained a moment you'd taken personally. What did you do? (Her details can stay private. Just what happened in you.)

That's what knowing her story does. It doesn't make you her therapist or give you the right to explain her to herself. It gives you your bearings. This moment might not be about you at all. And instead of fixing her, you can ask God, what does strength look like from me right now?

Sometimes it's listening longer than you want to. Sometimes it's just staying in the room. Sometimes it's praying for her when she doesn't know you are.

And here's the relief. You're not her rescuer. Jesus is. He was after her heart long before you showed up. You get to walk with Him while He does it.

No woman close to you right now? It still applies. Every woman you meet is His daughter, with a story you don't know.

So here's what I want you to carry today. Know her. Don't fix her.

It's Saturday. Take ten minutes with Dad and ask Him about her. What's her story? What does she need from me that isn't one more demand? How do You want me to love her here?

He knows her better than you ever will. Let Him show you.

Fierce for Her

Reading

Eight weeks home.

The last clip in the Beauty session was Les Misérables. Fantine has lost everything. Javert wants her locked up. She spits on Jean Valjean, and he stands between her and all of it anyway.

He needs nothing from her. He's fierce toward what's crushing her. Then he sits by her bed and tells her the truth: In His eyes, you've never been anything but an innocent and beautiful woman.

Fierce for her. Tender with her. Never fierce toward her.

That's the whole week in one scene. From taking to offering. From needing to bringing. From fixing to knowing. From using to loving.

Carry this
Fierce for her. Tender with her.
Audio script John

Every time I watch that Les Mis scene, I notice what Jean Valjean doesn't do.

He doesn't need anything from Fantine. She spits on him, and he stands up for her anyway. He's not working an angle. He's not scared of her. He's not trying to fix her. He shows up, takes on what's crushing her, and then he's gentle.

One of my brothers stopped showing up for a while. He missed a couple of ManAlive events. He stopped coming to our Sunday night meetings, because Sunday was the only night he could have date night with his wife.

For over a year, he threw himself into winning her heart back. Taking her on dates. Listening to her. Finding out what was going on inside her. Loving her.

Sure, he got something out of it as a husband. But that's not why he did it. He did it because he valued her. He wanted their marriage to be more honest and more intimate. And he gave up a lot to focus on that.

His wife felt it. And his sons gained a whole new respect for him. They watched how he treated their mom, and how hard he fought to get her back. He showed those boys something no book or lecture ever could.

That's bringing strength. It's not control. It's not knowing what's best for her. It's having enough of your heart anchored in Dad that you can show up for her without needing her to make you okay.

So here's what I want you to carry into this week. Fierce for her. Tender with her.

Fierce toward whatever is coming against her. Tender with her heart. Never fierce toward her.

And if this week stirred up things you'd rather have left alone, that's okay. You don't have to be finished. You turned toward Dad. That's the whole thing.

Today, rest. Read back through your notes. And sit with that question I left you. Who actually knows my heart? Not my roles. My heart.

We'll start there tomorrow. Fierce for her. Tender with her.
