Week 6 overview

What this week is for

Week 6 walks a man back through Saturday morning's Battle session, so he can use what he learned there on an ordinary Tuesday afternoon. Day 40 carries When the Battle Goes Dark, the session on suicide and staying with a brother. By Sunday he should be able to say:

There is a real battle, and I don't have to be obsessed with the enemy to see it. He comes at me through messages: accusation, diminishment, his spin on what happened. A wound and a message are not the same thing. An agreement is when I accept a false message as true and start living from it. I can learn to catch those. I have authority in Christ to stop agreeing with them. I know what to do: Recognize, Break Agreement, Ask for Truth, Listen. I don't have to keep running. I can turn around.

We Are at War

Reading

Saturday morning started with three trailers, back to back. Tears of the Sun. Fury. The Matrix.

Nobody had to explain them. The room got quiet in a different way.

Something is at stake. You have a role. Wake up.

Then the weekend turned. Friday was about what happened to your heart. Saturday said there's been another voice in that story the whole time, telling you what it all meant.

There's a real enemy. He comes to steal, kill, and destroy. Ignoring him doesn't get you out of the fight. It just means you fight it asleep.

And hear this too: we're not getting spooky. Nobody's hunting demons behind the couch. This isn't about fear. It's about clarity.

This week we walk back through that session. You learned a way to fight. You're going to use it.

Fury said it in three words. Do your job.

Let me tell you what I think that line means, because it can go sideways fast.

In Fury, somebody's yelling it over the noise of the fight. Do your job. And a lot of us hear that and think, okay. Try harder. Grit your teeth. Be a better man by Friday.

That's not it.

Your job isn't to become some spiritual commando. Your job isn't to chase the devil around your house. Your job is your heart. Above all else, guard your heart. Keep it. Fight for it.

Because here's the thing about this war. You don't get to opt out. You can ignore it. Plenty of men do. They figure life is hard, they're tired, and that's just how it is. But ignoring a fight doesn't mean you're not in one. It means you're losing ground and don't know why.

Now, not everything bad is the enemy. The world's broken. People make choices. Bodies get sick. Sometimes you fall off a ladder because you fell off a ladder.

But right after the bad thing, there's another voice. Telling you what it means. What it says about you. That's where the fight is. Not out there somewhere. In here. Over what you believe.

So this week we get practical. Saturday morning you were handed a way to fight, simple enough to use on a Tuesday afternoon. We're going to walk it again.

So here's what I want you to carry today. Do your job.

Not hustle. Not fear. Just pay attention to your heart today. Notice when something lands heavy. Notice what you start telling yourself about it.

Then take it to Dad. Ask Him where the fight is.

Do your job. Your job is your heart.

Day 37 · TuesdayAudio about 2:00

Real Event, False Spin

Reading

Remember the three words from Saturday morning? Wound. Message. Agreement.

The wound is what happened to your heart. The message is what you heard, or came to believe, through what happened. The agreement is when you accept that message as true and start living from it.

They're not the same thing. That matters.

Here's how it plays out. Your marriage is struggling. That's real. Then a message shows up: You're a terrible husband. Agree with it, and it becomes: I'm a terrible husband. I always screw this up. Now you're living from it. You quit trying. Or you try way too hard.

The event can be real. The pain can be real. Even the failure can be real. The accusation attached to it can still be a lie.

This week, start noticing the second voice. The one that shows up right after something happens and tells you what it means about you.

You don't have to fight it yet. Just catch it.

See how fast that was? The thing happened, and before I could even take a breath, the meaning was already there. And it sounded like me. It sounded like the truth.

That's how this works. Most of the time the enemy doesn't have to cause the thing. Life does that on its own. He just shows up afterward with his spin. He tells you what it means. And most of us have been taking his word for it for years.

Here's the difference that changes everything. The wound is what happened. The message is what you were told it means. Two different things. You can't change what happened. But you don't have to keep believing what you were told about it.

So here's what I want you to carry today. Real event. False spin.

When something hits today, and something will, let it be real. Don't pretend it didn't hurt. Then ask one question. Who's telling me what this means?

You don't have to fix it today. Thursday we'll walk through how you fight it. Today, just catch it.

Then take it to Dad. Ask Him what He says it means. He has a version too. And His is true.

Real event. False spin.

Robert Sat Down

Reading

Remember Robert the Bruce and his father?

Robert's heart is on fire. He's watched William Wallace fight, and he wants in. Then his father talks to him. Calm. Reasonable. Survive. Play it safe. Compromise. "Uncompromising men are easier to admire."

And you watch Robert start to give his heart away.

That's how the enemy works most days. Not with horns. With a voice that sounds wise. Or that sounds like you.

At camp we named his two favorite moves.

Accusation. Look what you did. You're disqualified. If these guys really knew you.

Diminishment. You don't have what it takes. You're too old. It's too late. Sit down. Stay safe.

After the clip came two questions. What did Robert agree with? And where does that happen in me?

You don't need a long list. You probably already know which one gets you.

Reading

Saturday morning you were handed a way to fight. Simple enough to use on a Tuesday afternoon.

Recognize the message. What am I hearing?

Find the agreement. Where have I accepted this as true?

Break it out loud. "I break the agreement that ______."

Ask Jesus for truth. "Jesus, what is the truth?"

Listen. Shut up long enough to hear. Write down what comes.

Out loud isn't magic. There's no secret formula in the words. But something happens when a man names what he's believed and says plainly, I don't agree with that anymore.

It takes courage. Remember Robin Hood? Rise and rise again. Breaking an agreement that feels true is about as brave as a man gets.

And the authority is real. It's Christ's, and He gave it to you as His son.

Some agreements break the first time. Some have been around for decades. You keep going.

When the Battle Goes Dark

Reading

Right after the Battle session at camp, we talked about something most men never say out loud. Suicide.

You watched Tyson Fury. The heavyweight champion of the world was thinking about suicide, and the people looking at him could not see the fight happening inside him. Strength didn't make him immune. Neither did success, or people who loved him. He needed help, and help came.

Men like us can struggle with this. Men who work, provide, laugh, and show up. You're allowed to say the word. And you can tell somebody.

If you're worried about a brother, ask him straight: Are you thinking about suicide? You don't have to fix him. Ask, and stay.

If it's you, don't wait. You don't need a speech. "I need to talk" is enough. Tell one person today.

Call or text 988, the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (in the US), any time, day or night. If you might act on it right now, call 988 or 911, or get to someone near you.

Decisions made in this draft

  • One section of the session per day, in camp order. Day 36 the trailers and "we are at war," Day 37 wound, message, agreement, Day 38 Braveheart with accusation and diminishment, Day 39 the breaking process, Day 41 The Matrix subway, Day 42 the landing. Standing in the Way of Freedom sits inside Days 36 and 38. Robin Hood gets one line on Day 39 ("Rise and rise again"), as the courage it takes to break an agreement that feels true.
  • When the Battle Goes Dark is one day, Day 40 (Friday), right after the practice day, the way it followed Battle at camp. It is a first-response and brotherhood day: men like us can struggle with this, say the word, tell somebody, ask directly, don't fix, stay, bring help close, follow up, he's still your brother. It uses the session's own five words (Ask. Stay. Safe. Connect. Follow.) and no other framework. No story slot. Tyson Fury is named only as hidden suffering; no details of his crisis.
  • 988 is in the Day 40 reading, audio, and email line. The reading also says "If you might act on it right now, call 988 or 911, or get to someone near you." That wording comes from ManAlive's own coach guidance for a man who discloses. Confirm you want 911 named on that page, or cut it to 988 only.
  • Placeholder to replace: "[Link: ManAlive suicide resources page, coming]" is a line in the Day 40 reading, so it will load into the app as-is if not replaced first. Swap in the real link before this week goes live.
  • No named contact on Day 40. Camp had the 1:00 conversation and named leaders. The app doesn't invent names. If you want a real ManAlive person or number listed, give it to me.
  • Locked language only: wound, message, agreement, truth; "I break the agreement that ______"; "Jesus, what is the truth?" No legal-contract or courtroom-of-heaven language (one older talk used it; the current spec rules it out). Out loud is "not magic."
  • Authority anchored in Christ (Day 39): "It's Christ's, and He gave it to you as His son." Not self-made, not swagger.
  • Saturday's 10 minutes is a short Battle Covenant of Silence, using its own questions: "Which event would You like to take me to? What message did I hear?"
  • Four story slots (Days 37, 38, 39, 41). Day 39 is your Battle story, the heart of the session: keep the event short, the agreement in one sentence, and don't end on "never struggled again." Days 36, 40, and 42 use coaching, the camp moment, and a Tuesday-afternoon picture.
  • Jail story: offered once, as an optional part of the Day 41 slot ("when you stopped running and turned around"). Use it only if it fits. The script works either way.
  • No held stories used. None were assigned to Week 6. Responders' stories from the transcripts (Chic, Dave, Steve) are not used.
  • Day 42 bridge: "If the battle isn't the whole story, what else did He make me for?" points toward An Adventure to Live without teaching it.
  • Day 40 reading runs about 180 words, longer than the others, to fit the 988 lines. Day 36's "Above all else, guard your heart" is Proverbs 4:23, unreferenced.
